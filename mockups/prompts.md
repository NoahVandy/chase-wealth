# Chase Wealth — Landing Page Design Prompts

Three styles were randomly selected (PowerShell `Get-Random` over the full 25-style list):
**Art Deco**, **Material**, **Neo-Geo**. Each prompt below is exactly three paragraphs, per the brief.

## 1. Art Deco — `art-deco.html`

Design in the **Art Deco** style and conceive an innovative private-wealth advisory concept for a single-page landing page — a modern boutique that treats financial stewardship as a craft object, rendered with the elegance of a grand hotel lobby distilled to its essential lines. The visitor should arrive and feel an immediate hush of occasion: vertical symmetry, a proscenium-like hero, and ornament that is earned rather than applied. As they scroll this one cohesive page, the rhythm should feel like descending a sweeping staircase — each section a landing, each transition deliberate, the hierarchy stepping down from monumental display type to fine engraved detail. Incorporate colorful elements sparingly and preciously: champagne gold and warm brass against deep near-black and cream, so that every glint of color reads as gilding on stone rather than decoration for its own sake.

The design philosophy is luxury through geometry. Typography should feel authoritative and ceremonial — tall, letterspaced capitals for proclamations, a quiet humanist text face for counsel — as if every headline were set in bronze above a doorway. Interactions should feel machined and weighty: slow, confident reveals, hairline rules that draw themselves, hover states that warm from ink to gold like metal catching lamplight — never bouncy, never fast. The single-page journey is a narrative arc from threshold to inner sanctum: the hero is the façade that promises substance, the middle sections are the appointed rooms where the craft is demonstrated, and the final call-to-action is the private study where the visitor is invited, by name, to sit down — a closing that should feel like an engraved invitation rather than a sales button.

For reference points, draw on the emotional quality of interwar grand architecture: the vertical thrust of stepped skyscraper crowns, the fanned sunburst of a theater proscenium, the repeated chevrons of an elevator door in polished brass. Channel the atmosphere of first-class ocean-liner salons, letterpress certificates and engraved banknotes, and the disciplined opulence of a jewelry vitrine where one object sits in a pool of light. These references should influence the page not as pastiche but as temperament — symmetry as trust, ornament as evidence of care, restraint as the highest form of wealth — so the final single-page design feels like a modern financial institution that remembers when institutions were built to last a century.

## 2. Material — `../index.html` + `../about.html` *(selected — promoted to the repo root)*

> This is the concept the client chose. It moved out of `mockups/` to become the real site:
> `index.html` (landing) and `about.html` (advisor bio). The brief below still governs the visual
> style and interaction feel; the copy on those pages is the client's real content, subject to the
> constraints in `CLAUDE.md` § "Client constraints". Note the shipped pages are black-and-white,
> not the "deep botanical green" described below.

Design in the **Material** style and conceive an innovative wealth-management platform concept for a single-page landing page — advice presented as a calm, intelligent surface, where every fact the visitor needs sits on its own card like a well-organized desk. The arrival mood is clarity and quiet competence: generous whitespace, a soft layered elevation system, and one confident accent color blooming against a disciplined neutral field. As the visitor scrolls the single cohesive page, content should feel like sheets of paper gently rising to meet them — cards lifting on approach, sections snapping into a legible grid — producing the emotional sensation that nothing here is hidden, nothing is decorative noise, and everything has a place. Incorporate colorful elements purposefully: a deep botanical green as the primary voice, with soft tonal surfaces derived from it, so color signals action and warmth without ever shouting.

The philosophy is paper-and-ink made physical: surfaces have honest depth, shadows describe real elevation, and motion explains rather than entertains. Typography should feel friendly but engineered — large, humane headlines with comfortable line-height, crisp labels in small caps, numbers presented like instrument readouts — welcoming to a first-time investor, credible to a seasoned one. Interactions should be snappy and precise with a soft landing: quick ripples of acknowledgment, cards that elevate a single step on hover, buttons that feel pressable, all easing with natural deceleration. The one-page journey progresses emotionally from orientation (a serene hero that states the promise plainly) through growing confidence (tangible cards of services, process, and proof) to resolution — a filled, high-emphasis call-to-action that feels like the obvious, frictionless next step rather than a leap.

Abstractly, channel the feeling of a beautifully run modern clinic or a well-designed public library: institutional trust expressed through wayfinding, light, and order instead of marble and dark wood. Reference the tactile satisfaction of quality stationery — smooth card stock, a rounded corner, ink that sits perfectly on the page — and the calm of interfaces designed by people who measured everything twice. Let the emotional quality of these environments — legibility as kindness, consistency as integrity, motion as explanation — govern the final single-page design, so the visitor leaves feeling that a firm this organized with pixels will be equally organized with their money.

## 3. Neo-Geo — `neo-geo.html`

Design in the **Neo-Geo** style and conceive an innovative quantitative-meets-human wealth advisory concept for a single-page landing page — a firm whose promise is that disciplined structure produces beautiful outcomes, expressed through refined geometric pattern and mathematical composition. The visitor should arrive into a space that feels like standing inside a well-proportioned diagram: a modular grid made visible, circles and quarter-arcs locking together like gears, headlines aligned to an invisible lattice that the eye can feel even when it cannot see it. Scrolling the single cohesive page should evoke the pleasure of a proof unfolding — each section a lemma building on the last, pattern density rising and resolving like musical phrasing. Incorporate colorful elements as controlled variables: a strict monochrome field punctuated by cobalt and a single warm signal color, deployed in flat geometric planes so each appearance of color feels like a data point placed with intent.

The design philosophy is rationalism with a pulse. Typography should feel precise and modern-geometric — circular bowls, uniform strokes, tabular numerals — authoritative the way a well-drawn axis is authoritative, never cold because the proportions themselves are generous. Interactions should be crisp and metronomic: elements translating along grid lines, arcs sweeping through exact degrees, hover states that swap a shape's fill like flipping a token — motion with the snap of a drafting instrument, not the wobble of jelly. The emotional arc of the single page moves from fascination (an abstract geometric hero that rewards a second look) through comprehension (services and process rendered as legible systems of shape) to conviction — a final call-to-action framed as the logical conclusion of everything above it, the QED of the page.

For reference, summon the emotional qualities of mid-century concrete-poetry posters, tiled courtyards where a simple module generates infinite variation, and the quiet awe of an observatory instrument — brass circles, etched scales, machinery built to measure the sky. Draw on the atmosphere of design-school foundation studios: compass, straightedge, flat color, and the discipline of doing more with less. These references should shape the final single-page design's emotional register — pattern as competence, geometry as honesty, repetition as reliability — so that the firm reads as one that finds order in markets the way these traditions found order in form.

## 4. Neumorphic — `neumorphic.html` *(strict black & white)*

Design in the **Neumorphic** style and conceive an innovative private-wealth advisory concept for a single-page landing page — finance rendered as a tactile instrument panel, every control and card gently extruded from a single continuous surface, executed entirely in black, white, and the grays between them. The visitor should arrive into a feeling of physical calm: nothing floats, nothing is pasted on; every element appears pressed out of or into the same soft material, like controls molded from one sheet of matte ceramic. As they scroll this one cohesive page, the hierarchy should be felt with the fingertips of the eye — raised surfaces invite touch, recessed wells hold information safely — producing an atmosphere of quiet containment, as if the visitor's finances were already resting somewhere padded, ordered, and secure. Because the palette is strictly monochrome, all emotional color must come from light itself: the interplay of soft highlight and soft shadow doing the work that hue would normally do.

The design philosophy is softness as trust. Typography should feel rounded, unhurried, and lowercase-friendly — labels engraved rather than printed, numbers sitting in dished recesses like coins in a tray — welcoming in the way well-worn tools are welcoming. Interactions should be gentle and organic: buttons that visibly depress when pressed and rise again on release, toggles that slide with cushioned ease, hover states that swell the extrusion by a breath rather than snapping. The single-page emotional journey moves from sensory reassurance (a hero that feels like resting a hand on something solid) through tactile exploration (services and process as a panel of touchable modules) to a final call-to-action that reads as the most pleasingly pressable object on the page — commitment framed not as a leap but as the satisfying click of a well-made switch.

For abstract reference, draw on the emotional quality of high-end monochrome industrial design: the seamless housing of a precision audio device, the dished keys of a beloved vintage keyboard, pebbles worn smooth to neutral gray, plaster walls raked by late light so that geometry appears only as shadow. Channel the hush of a recording-studio control room at night — matte surfaces, soft falloff, everything within reach and nothing shouting. These references should give the final single-page design its register of restrained physical luxury: a world with no color and no noise, where depth, texture, and the behavior of light carry the entire emotional argument that this firm is solid, soft-spoken, and built to be handled for decades.

## 5. Modernist — `modernist.html` *(strict black & white)*

Design in the **Modernist** style and conceive an innovative private-wealth advisory concept for a single-page landing page — advice presented with the clean lines and functional beauty of timeless design, in nothing but black ink on white ground. The visitor should arrive and feel immediate intellectual respect: a page that assumes their intelligence, states its case in large unadorned type, and wastes not one pixel on decoration that does not carry meaning. The mood is the confidence of things built to last — clarity as courtesy, structure as generosity. As they scroll the single cohesive page, the visual hierarchy should unfold like a well-argued essay: thesis in the hero, evidence in measured sections divided by honest rules, each block aligned to a grid the reader senses as inevitability rather than constraint. With color removed entirely, contrast becomes the only voice — scale against scale, black field against white field, density against emptiness — and every one of those contrasts must be deliberate.

The design philosophy is form follows function, felt emotionally rather than recited. Typography is the entire architecture: authoritative headlines set tight and large, body text at a comfortable measure, numerals treated as first-class citizens of the composition — the whole page readable at a glance and rewarding at a second one. Interactions should be almost invisible in their precision: underlines that arrive exactly on hover, blocks that invert from white-on-black to black-on-white like a page turning, transitions brief enough to register as responsiveness rather than performance. The one-page journey progresses from declaration (a hero that says the important thing plainly) through demonstration (services, method, and proof laid out with the neutrality of a well-set table) to decision — a closing call-to-action that is simply the largest, plainest, most honest object on the page, persuasive because everything before it was true.

For abstract reference, channel the emotional qualities of mid-century functionalist tradition: the poise of a perfectly set book page, furniture whose beauty is its joinery, buildings of glass and steel whose ornament is proportion itself. Recall the atmosphere of a great museum's typography — wayfinding that never raises its voice yet is never missed — and of design ateliers where "less, but better" was a moral position before it was a slogan. These references should shape the final single-page design toward permanence over fashion: black and white not as an absence of color but as a statement that the firm deals in fundamentals — legibility, proportion, truth in materials — and that money managed here will be managed the same way.

## 6. Minimalism — `minimalism.html`

Design in the **Minimalism** style and conceive an innovative private-wealth advisory concept for a single-page landing page — advice reduced to only what is essential, presented with so much calm and space that the little which remains feels deliberate and true. The visitor should arrive into quiet: vast margins, a single unhurried column of type, and the sense that nothing has been added merely to persuade them. As they scroll this one cohesive page, the experience should feel like turning the leaves of a finely made notebook — each section separated by air rather than ornament, the eye guided by emptiness and alignment instead of rules and boxes. Incorporate color with extreme restraint, if at all: a near-white ground, soft ink-gray text, and at most a single muted accent used no larger than a hairline, so that the near-absence of color reads as confidence rather than austerity.

The design philosophy is subtraction as respect — every element must justify its presence or be removed. Typography carries the entire page: a single humane sans in light and regular weights, generous line-height, headlines only a touch larger than body text so hierarchy is felt through space and position rather than size and volume. Interactions should be almost imperceptible: a link that quietly underlines, a section that fades gently into place, a button that shifts by a hair on hover — motion you notice mainly in its restraint. The one-page journey moves from stillness (a hero that states the promise in one plain sentence) through unhurried demonstration (services, method, and proof each given room to breathe) to a closing call-to-action that persuades precisely because it asks for so little and hides nothing.

For abstract reference, channel the emotional quality of a gallery's white wall, a single object lit on a wide shelf, the wide margin of a well-set book, and the hush of a tea room where every object earns its place. Recall the calm of Scandinavian interiors, the discipline of a stationer's blank notebook, and the design traditions where "less" was never a lack but a clarity. These references should shape the final single-page design toward serenity and trust — space as luxury, restraint as honesty, quiet as competence — so the visitor feels that a firm this careful about what to leave out will be equally careful about what to leave in their portfolio.

Interactions should be subtle and immaculate: pill buttons with a barely-perceptible lift, cards whose shadows deepen one step on hover, reveals that fade up a few pixels and settle without bounce — animation as a courtesy, never a performance. ## 8. Editorial Luxury — `editorial-luxury.html`

Design in the **Editorial Luxury** style and conceive an innovative private-wealth advisory concept for a single-page landing page — the firm presented as a high-end magazine feature about itself, composed with the unhurried confidence of a publication whose subject is generational wealth. The visitor should arrive at a masthead, not a homepage: hairline rules, a centered wordmark, an issue-like sense of occasion, and a hero headline set in a large, beautiful serif that would not be out of place opening a long-form profile. The palette is black ink on warm cream, with gold reserved for the finest gestures — a hairline rule, a folio numeral, an initial ornament — never poured. As the visitor scrolls the single cohesive page, sections should turn like well-art-directed spreads: wide margins, columns of measured serif text, engraved line-art plates (inline SVG standing in for photography) captioned like figures, and pull quotes given the room of a full spread. The atmosphere is lots of spacing and slow, deliberate motion — nothing on this page has ever hurried.

The design philosophy is permanence over promotion: the page should read as something printed, bound, and kept. Typography is the luxury — large serif display headlines with true italics for grace notes, a readable serif for body text, small-caps letterspaced labels doing the wayfinding — hierarchy expressed the way fine books express it, through size, style, and space rather than color and weight. Interactions should be slow and gracious: reveals that rise like a page settling, underlines that draw themselves at reading speed, a hover that warms black to gold as if catching lamplight — every transition long enough to feel considered, brief enough to never delay the reader. The single-page journey is an editorial arc: a cover-like hero that makes the thesis feel important, feature sections that document the firm's craft as if reporting on it, a testimonial treated as the profile's key quotation, and a closing invitation set like the magazine's final page — formal, engraved, addressed to the reader.

For abstract reference, draw on the emotional qualities of heirloom print: the front matter of a private bank's centenary volume, engraved wedding stationery and share certificates, the masthead and folio discipline of the great literary magazines, and the hush of a members' reading room with green-shaded lamps. Channel the restraint of luxury houses whose advertisements are mostly empty space and one perfect serif line. These references should shape the final single-page design toward inheritance rather than acquisition — cream as patience, black as authority, gold as evidence of occasion — so the firm reads as one that plans in decades and writes things down because the writing is meant to outlast everyone in the room.

## 9. Scandinavian — `scandinavian.html`

Design in the **Scandinavian** style and conceive an innovative private-wealth advisory concept for a single-page landing page — financial advice rendered with the calm, thoughtful honesty of the best Nordic interiors, a firm that feels less like a corporation and more like a well-made room filled with natural light. The visitor should arrive into warmth: a ground of beige and sand, type in soft near-black, surfaces the color of stone and unbleached paper, and one accent of warm wood used the way a single oak chair warms a plaster room. Shapes are gentle — arches, rounded corners, generous air — and any texture is quiet and material: a fine wood-grain line pattern (inline SVG standing in for photography and physical texture) appearing only where a surface would honestly be wood. As the visitor scrolls the single cohesive page, sections should feel like moving through a bright, uncluttered home — each room prepared, nothing staged — producing the sensation that this firm has nothing to hide because there is nowhere for anything to hide.

The design philosophy is honest materials, human scale: nothing pretends to be what it isn't, and comfort is achieved through proportion rather than decoration. Typography should feel warm but disciplined — a soft, slightly humanist serif for headlines, an open friendly sans for text, sizes that never shout — the voice of someone explaining something clearly at a kitchen table. Interactions should be calm and tactile: reveals that ease in like daylight moving across a floor, cards that lift almost imperceptibly on hover, a button press with a soft, sure response — motion with the temperament of a well-oiled drawer. The single-page journey moves from welcome (a hero that feels like being invited in) through unhurried explanation (services and method as tidy, well-crafted furnishings) to an ending that feels like being offered a seat — a contact section framed as a standing invitation to talk, plainly and without obligation.

For abstract reference, channel the emotional quality of contemporary Nordic design culture: the domestic warmth of a design house's showroom, the apothecary calm of a shop where every label is typeset with care, pale timber floors, linen, stoneware, and low afternoon sun through large windows. Recall the ethic beneath the aesthetic — lagom, "just enough" — where quality is proven by how little needs to be added and how long things are kept. These references should shape the final single-page design toward calm, thoughtfulness, and honesty — sand as patience, wood as warmth, light as transparency — so the visitor feels that a firm this comfortable being plain about its rooms will be equally plain about its fees, its rules, and their money.
